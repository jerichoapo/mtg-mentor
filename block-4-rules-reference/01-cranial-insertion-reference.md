# Cranial Insertion

**Weekly rules Q&A written by Magic judges. Published from 2005 until July 2025.**

> Why it's in the list: twenty years of weekly rules Q&A written by judges, searchable by card and
> interaction. The layers, priority, and state-based-action questions are where Arena players get caught
> in paper, because the client resolves all of it silently. **Consult it, don't read through it.**

## What it is

**Weekly rules Q&A, published Sunday night / Monday morning at midnight Eastern**, running from 2005.
Written by working judges, in a deliberately light register — the site's own description promises you'll
be **"educated by having fuzzy bunnies shoved into your head."**

**Each issue is a batch of reader-submitted questions with rulings and explanations.** Issues are
translated into **German and French** alongside English.

## Why it earns a slot in a strategy reading list

**Everything else in this library is about deciding what to do. This is about what actually happens when
you do it.**

**The specific gap it fills, per the reading-list annotation:** **layers, priority, and state-based
actions.** Magic Arena resolves all three silently and correctly, which means a player who learned the
game digitally has never had to construct those interactions by hand. In paper, you and your opponent are
the game engine.

**Cross-reference to where the library sets this up:**

- **Priority** — the mechanics of who acts when, and the fact that the active player must act first, are
  laid out in `block-1-the-spine/04-attacking-and-blocking-2015-07-27.md`. Duke's combat-phase walkthrough
  is the minimum you need before consulting rulings.
- **The stack, and what is and isn't a spell** — `block-1-the-spine/12-permission-spells-2015-08-17.md`.
  Duke's precise definitions there (lands never use the stack; a permanent is not a spell; you can only
  counter something currently on the stack) are exactly the distinctions Cranial Insertion adjudicates in
  detail.
- **Casting a spell correctly** — `block-1-the-spine/25-going-through-the-motions-2015-05-11.md`. Duke's
  four-step announcement procedure, and his story about illegally skipping the colour choice on Center
  Soul, is the practical reason this reference matters.
- **Calling a judge** — `block-1-the-spine/44-playing-in-a-grand-prix-part-ii-2015-05-26.md`. Duke's
  advice is to never take your opponent's word on an unclear rule. This archive is the at-home version of
  that habit.

## How to use it

**Do not read it front to back.** Twenty years of weekly Q&A is not a course, it's a corpus.

**The workflow that makes it useful:**

1. **When an interaction confuses you mid-game, note it down.** Duke's own habit, from installment 44:
   *"Any time I encounter a question while I'm at home, I make a note to ask a judge about it at my next
   Grand Prix."* The archive is the version of that you can do immediately.
2. **Search by card name or by mechanic**, not by issue. The value is in retrieval, not in browsing.
3. **Check the issue date against the cards involved.** A ruling from 2011 may predate a rules change or a
   later card that interacts differently. Layers and state-based actions have been revised more than once.
4. **For anything after mid-2025, look elsewhere.** The archive stops.

## What to look up first

If you're moving from Arena to paper, the interactions worth reading proactively rather than reactively:

- **The layer system** — how continuous effects apply in order, and why the sequence in which two effects
  were created can change the result.
- **State-based actions** — when creatures die, when players lose, when tokens cease to exist, and the
  fact that these are checked automatically rather than announced.
- **Priority and the stack** — response windows, holding priority, and what happens when both players pass.
- **Replacement vs. triggered effects** — the distinction that determines whether something uses the stack
  and can be responded to.
- **Combat damage ordering and blocking requirements** — the rules underneath the tactical material in
  Block 1.

## Notes for use

- **This is the only reference in the library that is purely mechanical.** Everything in Blocks 0, 1, 2,
  3, and 5 is a claim about how to play well. This one is a record of how the game works, which is a
  different kind of knowledge and cannot be reasoned out from strategy.
- **The Arena-to-paper gap is real and worth taking seriously.** The client never lets you make an illegal
  play, never forgets a trigger, and never lets you miss a state-based action. Paper does all three, and
  the penalties in a tournament are real. See the Warnings section of
  `block-1-the-spine/44-playing-in-a-grand-prix-part-ii-2015-05-26.md`.
- **Judge-written rulings are authoritative in a way that strategy writing is not.** The rest of this
  library is argument; this is adjudication. Where a Cranial Insertion ruling and your intuition disagree,
  your intuition is wrong.
- **Archive it if you rely on it.** The site is on indefinite hiatus with no stated end date, and
  twenty-year-old hobbyist sites do disappear — as The Dojo did, which is why half of Block 2 exists only
  as reprints and Wayback captures. If specific rulings matter to you, save them locally.
