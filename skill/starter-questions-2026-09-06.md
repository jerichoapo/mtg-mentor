# What to ask the mentor

Questions the corpus can answer well, grouped by what you actually want. Phrase them
however you like; these are here to show where the depth is.

---

## Diagnose a loss

The highest-value use, and the one people reach for last. You describe what happened and
the mentor names the concept, then applies it. You do not need the vocabulary first.

- I keep losing to aggro with my control deck. What am I getting wrong?
- I had a dominant board and still lost. What should I be looking for in that game?
- My opponent and I both durdled for ten turns and I lost the long game. Was that
  decided at deck construction?
- I win game one and lose the next two constantly. What is that pattern usually?
- I mulliganed to five and won, then kept a sketchy seven and lost. How should I think
  about that?
- Why do I always seem to be the one reacting?
- I conceded a game I probably could have won because I was tilted about a misplay two
  turns earlier. Is that a known failure mode?

## Learn a concept

Twenty-four concepts have hand-built lineage entries. Ask for any of them plainly.

- What is inevitability and how do I know who has it?
- Explain tempo like I have never heard the word.
- What does "virtual card advantage" actually mean in practice?
- What is blanking, and how do I tell if a card in my hand is blank?
- What is the Philosophy of Fire?
- What is the fundamental turn and why does it matter to a control deck?
- What is line-up theory?
- What does "investment" mean when people talk about a card costing you tempo?
- Explain the difference between threats and answers as a deckbuilding idea.
- What are Timmy, Johnny and Spike, and is that framework still useful?

## Settle an argument or check an attribution

Where the corpus is uniquely strong, because it documents twelve cases where a widely
repeated citation is wrong.

- Who actually coined inevitability?
- Who invented virtual card advantage?
- Who wrote Shoulders of Giants?
- Is "Who's the Beatdown?" still correct, or has it been superseded?
- What does high-water mark really mean?
- Did anyone ever push back on the idea that card advantage decides everything?
- What is the Grand Unified Theory and why can I never find the actual article?
- Has Flores ever retracted anything he wrote?
- Where did the term Turbo Xerox come from?

## Manabase math

The most immediately actionable material in the library. Karsten's tables are transcribed
and verified.

- How many lands should I run for a deck with an average mana value of 2.8?
- How many black sources do I need to cast a 1BB three-drop on curve?
- How many green sources for a GG two-drop in a 99-card deck?
- Do fetchlands count as a full source? What about Evolving Wilds?
- How many taplands is too many in an aggro deck?
- How much does a mana dork count toward my colored sources?
- Can I cut lands if my deck is full of cheap cantrips?

## Decide what to read

- I am new to strategy theory. What should I read first, and in what order?
- What should I read about sideboarding?
- I have read Who's the Beatdown. What next?
- What is worth reading if I only have an hour?
- Which articles have aged badly, and which are still exactly right?

## Check whether something still applies

- I read an old piece whose example turns on mana burn. Is the rest of it still good?
- Is Karsten's land formula still right, or has the game moved on?
- This article is from 1997. Is any of it usable?

## What it will decline

It will say so rather than bluff, which is by design.

- Anything Commander. The corpus is one-on-one constructed and limited.
- Individual card evaluations, prices, and legality. Use `mtg-budget-deckbuilder`.
- Current formats. The newest strategy article is 2018, with a hard stop at 2025.
- Rules adjudication beyond a pointer to Cranial Insertion.

**Three questions land on real holes**, and it will tell you the primary source is lost
rather than reconstruct it: Zvi's formal definition of tempo, line-up theory in its
original form, and the applied Rule of Reflection. All three lived in the three paywalled
*Advantage Theory* installments.

---

## The best single question

**"Is card advantage actually the most important thing in Magic, or is that just something
people repeat?"**

It is a real question, the corpus contains a genuine multi-author argument about it, and
the honest answer is no. It shows what the library is for better than any concept
lookup does.
