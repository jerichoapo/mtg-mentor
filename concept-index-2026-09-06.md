# Concept index

**Every major idea in the library, traced through the corpus in the order the argument actually developed.**

The blocks give you a reading order. This gives you the other axis: pick an idea, see who introduced it, who
developed it, who corrected it, and where it sits today. Built 2026-09-06 against the 74-article corpus.

**How to use it.** Each entry runs chronologically, so the first line is the origin and the last line is the
current state of the argument. Where an entry says *corrected*, a later writer changed or retracted
something earlier, and reading only the older piece will leave you with the wrong idea.

**What this is not.** Block 1's [Glossary of Terms](block-1-the-spine/46-glossary-of-terms-2015-06-21.md)
defines about ninety terms and maps each to the Level One installment that teaches it. That glossary stops
at the edge of its own block. This index crosses all six.

# Resources

## Card advantage

*Having more useful cards than your opponent, and converting that into a win.*

1. **1996, Robert Hahn**, [Schools of Magic](block-2-core-theory-paired/00-schools-of-magic-1996-04-10.md).
   Documents the **Weissman school**, whose deck is built to win on card advantage and nothing else, and
   compresses every resource in the game to **life, cards, and mana**.
2. **2004, Zvi Mowshowitz**,
   [Advantage: The Grand Unified Theory](block-5-advanced-synthesis/01-advantage-the-grand-unified-theory-2004-02-09.md).
   **Corrected.** Zvi's charge against every prior model is that they *"treat cards as worth one unit and
   they begin when the game begins."* Both halves are wrong: cards differ in value, and the process starts
   at deck construction.
3. **2010, Patrick Chapin**,
   [The Theory of Everything](block-5-advanced-synthesis/07-innovations-the-theory-of-everything-2010-01-12.md).
   Reduces the game to three resource types, closing the loop Hahn opened in 1996.
4. **2015, Reid Duke**,
   [Level One 03](block-1-the-spine/03-the-basics-of-card-advantage-2015-07-13.md). The teachable version.
   Start here if the term is new.

**Read first:** Duke. **Then:** Zvi part 1, which is the best critique of the naive version ever written.

## Cardboard advantage

*Counting physical cards in hand and in play, and nothing else.*

Zvi's deliberate renaming of what its author called "pure card advantage," in
[part 1](block-5-advanced-synthesis/01-advantage-the-grand-unified-theory-2004-02-09.md). The rename is the
argument: *"It makes it much easier to understand what this system is, and what it is not."*

He grades it honestly, five strengths against eight failures. It counts dead cards, counts nullified cards,
misses tokens, misses flashback in the graveyard, and treats Mon's Goblin Raiders as equal to Rorix
Bladewing. **Its value is as a beginner's tool and as a demonstration of why better tools were needed.**

## Virtual card advantage (VCA)

*Counting a card your opponent cannot usefully cast as though it were not there.*

1. **Origin: Eric Taylor**, credited by Zvi as the first source of the theory.
2. **2004, Zvi**, [part 1](block-5-advanced-synthesis/01-advantage-the-grand-unified-theory-2004-02-09.md).
   Gives the formal definition and grades it: seven strengths, eight holes. Better than cardboard advantage,
   still treats every live card as identical.
3. **2004, Zvi**, [part 2](block-5-advanced-synthesis/02-advantage-vca-and-tempo-2004-02-16.md).
   **Corrected.** Demolishes the claim that *"every game is won with VCA"* with a two-word edit: **every game
   is won with Advantage.** Five counterexamples where something other than cards decided the game.
4. **2015, Reid Duke**, [Level One 03](block-1-the-spine/03-the-basics-of-card-advantage-2015-07-13.md) and
   [15](block-1-the-spine/15-threats-and-answers-2014-09-08.md). The working version.

**The trap Zvi names:** *"Losing the game is causing you to lose VCA,"* not the reverse. Any metric that
collapses precisely when you are already behind explains nothing. This is the single most portable idea in
the corpus and it applies well outside Magic.

## Blanking

*Making a card functionally useless, whether by the board state or by deck construction.*

Defined formally by Zvi in
[part 2](block-5-advanced-synthesis/02-advantage-vca-and-tempo-2004-02-16.md), with a checkable test:
a card is blank if, knowing both decks, **you would trade it for a literally blank card.**

The exclusion matters more than the rule. A card you have no time for is not blank unless it can never win
you the game. *"Even if I have to draw ten perfect cards in a row to get out of this mess, as long as this
card will end up winning me games I would otherwise lose it is not blank."*

Appears applied in [Level One 09](block-1-the-spine/09-control-decks-2014-10-06.md) and
[40](block-1-the-spine/40-sideboard-plans-2015-03-09.md), and it is the mechanism behind the burn deck's
strategy in [Zvi's Philosophy of Fire coda](block-5-advanced-synthesis/04-grand-unified-theory-philosophy-of-fire-2004-05-03.md).

## Card impact

*That cards differ in effect, and the difference has to enter the equation.*

Zvi's term, coined against a rival's misuse of "card quality." He is explicit that he had the terminology
wrong himself until a conversation corrected him, in
[part 2](block-5-advanced-synthesis/02-advantage-vca-and-tempo-2004-02-16.md). This is the hole in every
system he grades in [part 1](block-5-advanced-synthesis/01-advantage-the-grand-unified-theory-2004-02-09.md):
both cardboard advantage and VCA treat every live card as worth exactly one.

## Investment

*The cards you commit to the board before they have done anything for you.*

1. **1998, Mike Flores**, [Advanced Strategy: Investment](block-3-manabase-reference/00-investment-1998-03-20.md).
   The origin. Separates cards that **replace themselves** from cards that cost you a card up front, and
   fixes the accounting error underneath: **a permanent that has not yet generated value is not a card, it
   is a promise.**
2. **2004, Zvi**, [part 1](block-5-advanced-synthesis/01-advantage-the-grand-unified-theory-2004-02-09.md).
   Defends the term against misuse and updates it: *"Most modern decks do almost no investing, because they
   don't have that kind of time."*
3. **2015, Reid Duke**, [Level One 22](block-1-the-spine/22-investment-2015-03-02.md).

**Attribution conflict, documented:** Flores's 2013 retrospective credits the Team CMU players, notably
Andrew Cuneo and Erik Lauer. The 1998 post itself thanks Hahn, Kusumoto, Shuler, Taylor and Wakefield.

## The Rule of Reflection

*Everything in Magic has value equal to the amount it increases your chance to win the game.*

Zvi's own contribution and the standard the whole Advantage Theory series is measured against, stated in
[part 1](block-5-advanced-synthesis/01-advantage-the-grand-unified-theory-2004-02-09.md). Two things about
it are worth knowing: he **only found the right words after finishing all five parts**, and **Justin Gary**
corrected his phrasing.

*"If it doesn't make you more likely to win the game, it has no value. If it does, it has value equal to the
probability it will make the difference. Once you hear it that's an easy thing to say, but a difficult thing
to properly apply."*

**Everything else in the corpus is a tool for approximating this well enough to use at a table.** The
applied treatment was part 3, which is
[one of the three installments that could not be recovered](block-5-advanced-synthesis/03-advantage-theory-parts-3-5-unrecovered-2004-03-01.md).

## The three resources

*Life, cards, and mana, and the exchange rates between them.*

1. **1996, Hahn**, [Schools of Magic](block-2-core-theory-paired/00-schools-of-magic-1996-04-10.md).
   Compresses the game's resources to those three, nineteen years before Level One.
2. **2004, Zvi**, [part 1](block-5-advanced-synthesis/01-advantage-the-grand-unified-theory-2004-02-09.md).
   Names four: cards, tempo and speed, life, and **information**. Reducible to fewer when the board allows.
3. **2004, Zvi**, [the Philosophy of Fire coda](block-5-advanced-synthesis/04-grand-unified-theory-philosophy-of-fire-2004-05-03.md).
   Works out an actual exchange rate, with Shock as the unit. Contains the non-linearity that makes burn
   work: **"Life is the resource that only matters when you're about to run out of it."**
4. **2010, Chapin**, [The Theory of Everything](block-5-advanced-synthesis/07-innovations-the-theory-of-everything-2010-01-12.md).
   The explicit endpoint. Chapin says outright he is taking Zvi's theory to its inevitable extreme.

# Roles and time

## Tempo

*Time as a resource, and mana as the way it is spent.*

1. **1997, Eric Taylor**, [it's about TIME](block-2-core-theory-paired/04-its-about-time-1997-08-11.md).
   The origin: mana and time are the same resource seen two ways.
2. **2004, Zvi**, [part 2](block-5-advanced-synthesis/02-advantage-vca-and-tempo-2004-02-16.md).
   Reviews Taylor and Chad Ellis, and **widens the definition**: tempo usually shows up as life loss, but it
   *"can be reflected in any of the game's other resources over time."* Flags that the formal definition was
   coming in part 5. **That part is lost.**
3. **2014 to 2015, Reid Duke**, [Level One 05](block-1-the-spine/05-tempo-2015-07-20.md) and
   [06](block-1-the-spine/06-tempo-and-card-advantage-a-delicate-balance-2014-11-17.md).

**Note the gap.** Zvi's promised formal definition never survived, so Duke's treatment is the most complete
one the corpus actually contains. His rule of thumb from part 2 stands in for it: *"A net loss of mana or the
loss of an opportunity to get ahead on the effective amount of mana spent should invite an evaluation of the
game in terms of tempo."*

## The fundamental turn

*The turn on which the aggressive deck is trying to win, which sets everyone else's deadline.*

1. **1997, Eric Taylor**, [it's about TIME](block-2-core-theory-paired/04-its-about-time-1997-08-11.md).
2. **2000, Zvi Mowshowitz**,
   [Clear the Land and the Fundamental Turn](block-2-core-theory-paired/05-clear-the-land-and-the-fundamental-turn-2000-12-31.md).
   The named statement.
3. **2012, Mike Flores**,
   [Eight Core Principles](block-2-core-theory-paired/02-eight-core-principles-of-whos-the-beatdown-2012-03-16.md).
   Folds it into the beatdown framework as the clock that decides who has to act.
4. **2014, Duke**, [Level One 07](block-1-the-spine/07-linear-strategies-2014-12-29.md).

## Who's the beatdown / role assignment

*Which player has to be the aggressor in this matchup, and what happens when both get it wrong.*

1. **1999, Mike Flores**, [Who's the Beatdown?](block-2-core-theory-paired/01-whos-the-beatdown-1999-04-13.md).
   Names the two roles. The most famous article in the corpus.
2. **2003, Zvi**, [Who's the Beatdown II: Multitasking](block-2-core-theory-paired/03-whos-the-beatdown-ii-multitasking-2003-02-20.md).
   **Corrected, and Flores rates this above his own original.** The two roles are contested, contextual and
   reversible, a player can hold **both at once**, and the real question is not which role you have but
   *"what each player needs to accomplish to win."*
3. **2012, Flores**, [Eight Core Principles](block-2-core-theory-paired/02-eight-core-principles-of-whos-the-beatdown-2012-03-16.md).
   Rebuilds the theory formally and credits Zvi for the vocabulary. **Retracts his own third diagnostic
   question from 1999.**
4. **2015, Duke**, [Level One 17](block-1-the-spine/17-role-assignment-2015-01-05.md).

**Read all three of the first group in order.** This is the clearest lineage in the corpus: claim, correction,
reconstruction.

## Inevitability

*Who wins the long game from the current position if nothing changes.*

1. **2003, Zvi**, [Who's the Beatdown II](block-2-core-theory-paired/03-whos-the-beatdown-ii-multitasking-2003-02-20.md).
   **Coined here**, with a formal definition and the caution that it is a good kind of vague. It is
   **positional, not fixed**, so it changes hands mid-game, and it comes in degrees.
2. **2004, Zvi**, [part 2](block-5-advanced-synthesis/02-advantage-vca-and-tempo-2004-02-16.md). Applied:
   a deck with inevitability can afford to **give back the resource it has a surplus of** to avoid losing on
   tempo.
3. **2012, Flores**, [Eight Core Principles](block-2-core-theory-paired/02-eight-core-principles-of-whos-the-beatdown-2012-03-16.md).
   Adopts the term and credits Zvi.
4. **2014, Duke**, [Level One 16](block-1-the-spine/16-inevitability-2014-12-08.md).

## The Philosophy of Fire

*Burn as a strategy that opts out of the card war entirely.*

1. **2004-04-23, Mike Flores**, [The Philosophy of Fire](block-2-core-theory-paired/06-the-philosophy-of-fire-2004-04-23.md).
   The claim.
2. **2004-05-03, Zvi**, [Grand Unified Theory: Philosophy of Fire](block-5-advanced-synthesis/04-grand-unified-theory-philosophy-of-fire-2004-05-03.md).
   Ten days later, works out the exchange rates underneath it, with Shock as the baseline unit. Defines what
   a Fire deck actually is: *"a bunch of horrendously bad cards"* whose value comes from invalidating the
   opponent's strategy rather than from the cards themselves.
3. **2012, Flores**, [2/3/4 and the high-water mark](block-2-core-theory-paired/07-2-3-4-the-philosophy-of-fire-and-the-high-water-mark-2012-07-27.md).
   Restates it more cleanly and **credits Adrian Sullivan** with the original insight rather than himself.

## The high-water mark

*A property of a format: the damage threshold its common removal reaches, which sets the toughness a
creature needs to survive.*

Flores, [2/3/4](block-2-core-theory-paired/07-2-3-4-the-philosophy-of-fire-and-the-high-water-mark-2012-07-27.md).

**This one is widely misquoted**, including in the reading list this corpus was built from, as meaning the
peak position you reached and whether you converted it. That is not what it means. It is a fact about the
format, not about your game.

# Deckbuilding

## Mana curve and land count

1. **2015, Duke**, [Level One 02](block-1-the-spine/02-the-basics-of-mana-2015-07-06.md) and
   [37](block-1-the-spine/37-building-a-mana-base-2014-11-24.md). The qualitative treatment.
2. **2017, Frank Karsten**, [How Many Lands Do You Need](block-3-manabase-reference/02-how-many-lands-do-you-need-2017-05-30.md).
   The quantitative one, with full probability tables and the regression `lands = 16 + 3.14 × average CMC`.
3. **2018, Karsten**, [How Many Colored Mana Sources](block-3-manabase-reference/03-how-many-colored-mana-sources-do-you-need-2018-10-10.md).
   The companion. The land count and the colour split are coupled, and neither article is usable alone.
4. **Tool:** [the calculator](block-3-manabase-reference/04-karsten-mana-calculator-reference.md).

**Karsten's own caveat is the part people drop:** the tables are *"useful guidelines only,"* and he points
at Pro Tour winning decks that ran below his own threshold.

## Turbo Xerox

*Cutting lands because cheap self-replacing card selection does the work lands would have done.*

**1997, Alan Comer**, [Turbo Xerox](block-3-manabase-reference/01-turbo-xerox-1997-06-27.md). It is
[Investment](#investment) applied to the manabase, which is why the two sit adjacent in Block 3.

**Two corrections carried in that file:** the URL most citations use points at a different article entirely,
and **"Xerox" refers to photocopying a cheap decklist**, not to cards copying themselves.

## Threats and answers

1. **1996, Hahn**, [Schools of Magic](block-2-core-theory-paired/00-schools-of-magic-1996-04-10.md).
   Present as the **Active versus Reactive** split.
2. **2004, Zvi**, [part 1](block-5-advanced-synthesis/01-advantage-the-grand-unified-theory-2004-02-09.md).
   Sharpens it: **threat status is a modelling decision, not a property of the card.** Which of your
   opponent's cards you decide to respect determines your whole read of the position.
3. **2014, Duke**, [Level One 15](block-1-the-spine/15-threats-and-answers-2014-09-08.md).

## Line-up theory

*Comparing the threats in one deck against the answers in another to see who is actually ahead.*

Attributed to Zvi's Advantage Theory series, and this is a **documented gap**. The term does not appear in
parts 1 or 2, so it belongs to
[parts 3 through 5, which are lost](block-5-advanced-synthesis/03-advantage-theory-parts-3-5-unrecovered-2004-03-01.md).
Zvi uses burn *"as a form of lineup"* in
[Who's the Beatdown II](block-2-core-theory-paired/03-whos-the-beatdown-ii-multitasking-2003-02-20.md) a year
earlier.

**The surviving full treatment is Duke**, [Level One 23](block-1-the-spine/23-line-up-theory-2015-03-23.md).

## Symmetry

1. **1996, Hahn**, [Schools of Magic](block-2-core-theory-paired/00-schools-of-magic-1996-04-10.md).
2. **1998, Flores**, [Investment](block-3-manabase-reference/00-investment-1998-03-20.md). The Howling Mine

3. **2014, Duke**, [Level One 14](block-1-the-spine/14-symmetric-effects-2014-09-01.md).

1. **1996, Hahn**, [Schools of Magic](block-2-core-theory-paired/00-schools-of-magic-1996-04-10.md).

2. **2003, Zvi**, [Who's the Beatdown II](block-2-core-theory-paired/03-whos-the-beatdown-ii-multitasking-2003-02-20.md).

# The player and the field

## Psychographics

*Timmy, Johnny and Spike: what a player is playing for.*

1. **2002, Mark Rosewater**, [Timmy, Johnny, and Spike](block-0-orientation/01-timmy-johnny-and-spike-2002-03-11.md).
2. **2006, Rosewater**, [Revisited](block-0-orientation/03-timmy-johnny-and-spike-revisited-2006-03-20.md).
   **The deepest of the three.** Adds the subgroups that make the framework diagnostic rather than merely
   descriptive.
3. **2025, Rosewater**, [The Three Magic Psychographics](block-0-orientation/02-the-three-magic-psychographics-2025-05-26.md).
   The current statement.

## The metagame

1. **2006, Patrick Chapin**, [Information Cascades](block-5-advanced-synthesis/05-information-cascades-in-magic-2006-06-28.md).
   Why the field converges on wrong answers.
2. **2015, Duke**, [Level One 41](block-1-the-spine/41-choosing-your-deck-2015-06-08.md) and
   [42](block-1-the-spine/42-the-metagame-2015-06-01.md). Duke summarises Chapin directly in 42.

## Information cascades

*A chain of decisions where everyone is copying the people ahead of them rather than acting on what they know.*

**2006, Chapin**, [Information Cascades in Magic](block-5-advanced-synthesis/05-information-cascades-in-magic-2006-06-28.md).
StarCityGames' 2006 Article of the Year, written before he was even a columnist there.

The failure condition, precisely: *"Once an individual stops relying on his own information and starts
imitating the group, the cascade stops being informative."* The compressed version: **imitation benefits the
individual now, innovation benefits the group eventually.**

**Watch what he does not claim.** He never says netdecking is bad. He says it usually wins more games for the
individual and usually costs the group information. Most restatements drop the first half.

## The cool-things problem

*Losing a won game because you were attached to a better-looking line.*

**1999, Chad Ellis**, [The Danger of Cool Things](block-2-core-theory-paired/08-the-danger-of-cool-things-1999-01-01.md).
The counterweight to everything else in Block 2, which is otherwise entirely about finding better lines.

His rule is the most immediately useful habit in the corpus: **when you improve a plan at the last minute,
re-check why the original was built that way.** Complements Duke on
[playing from ahead](block-1-the-spine/24-playing-from-ahead-playing-from-behind-2015-03-30.md), where the
instruction is to take the line with the fewest ways to lose rather than the best one.

## Where the corpus has holes

Three concepts in this index are **incompletely represented**, and all three trace to the same cause.

- **The formal definition of tempo.** Promised in Advantage Theory part 5.
- **Line-up theory in its original form.** Introduced somewhere in parts 3 through 5.
- **The Rule of Reflection applied.** That was the whole subject of part 3.

All three were Brainburst Premium and are paywalled in every surviving capture. The full record is
[block-5-advanced-synthesis/03](block-5-advanced-synthesis/03-advantage-theory-parts-3-5-unrecovered-2004-03-01.md).
