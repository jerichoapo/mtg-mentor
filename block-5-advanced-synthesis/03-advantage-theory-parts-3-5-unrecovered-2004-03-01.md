# Advantage Theory, parts 3-5: the unrecovered installments

**Zvi Mowshowitz — Brainburst (Premium), February 23 to March 1, 2004.**

> **This file is a dossier, not a reconstruction.** Parts one and two of the series are in this block at
> [01](01-advantage-the-grand-unified-theory-2004-02-09.md) and
> [02](02-advantage-vca-and-tempo-2004-02-16.md), recovered in full. Parts three, four and five are
> paywalled in every capture that exists anywhere on the public web. This records what they are, what the
> surviving parts say they contain, and exactly which retrieval paths were tried, so the search does not
> have to be repeated from scratch.

## The series, correctly identified

The single most useful thing found during this hunt is that **the series everyone calls "The Grand Unified
Theory" is not called that, and is not one article.** Brainburst ran it under the running head **"Advantage
Theory"**, and the in-series navigation block (preserved on the paywalled capture of part five) lists the
five parts by these names:

| Part | In-series title | Listing title | Date | Brainburst ID | Status |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | The Grand Unified Theory | Advantage: The Grand Unified Theory | 2004-02-09 | 3750 | **Recovered in full** |
| 2 | Virtual Card Advantage and Tempo | The Grand Unified Theory: Advantage - VCA and Tempo | 2004-02-16 | 3777 | **Recovered in full** |
| 3 | The Rule of Reflection | Advantage Theory - The Rule of Reflection Applied | 2004-02-23 | 3798 | **Not recovered** |
| 4 | Land Effect Analysis | Advantage Theory - Land Effect Analysis | 2004-02-26 | 3801 | **Not recovered** |
| 5 | Conclusion | Advantage Theory - Putting it all Together | 2004-03-01 | 3824 | **Not recovered** |

**Two related articles, also unrecovered:**

| Article | Date | ID | Status |
|---|---|---|---|
| What's in a Card? | 2004-05-06 | 4033 | Not recovered (paywalled, one capture) |
| The Grand Unified Theory | 2004-11-29 | 3992 | Not recovered — **the consolidated whole** |

**The 2004-11-29 article is the important one.** Contemporary accounts describe the series as "officially
published as a whole in November of 2004," running to more than sixty pages in print. That consolidated
version is ID 3992, and every capture of it is behind the same paywall.

**One article in the sequence is recovered and is not part of the series:**
[04: Grand Unified Theory - Philosophy of Fire](04-grand-unified-theory-philosophy-of-fire-2004-05-03.md),
published 2004-05-03, six weeks after part five ended. It is a coda applying the framework to Flores, and
it is the article the reading list originally pointed at.

## Scale

Brainburst's own promotional copy, preserved on the paywalled captures:

> **While classical card advantage formulas fall short, Zvi's vision never wavers. In this 15,000+ word
> epic, Zvi redefines the terms used in modern Magic theory and sets the new guidelines for discussion and
> evaluation. Zvi also gives definitive equations for advantage calculation, complete with the understanding
> needed to apply them.**

And from part one's listing:

> **Over the years, there have been countless attempts to distill the game of Magic down to its fundamental
> parts, and ultimately all of them have failed... Now, for the first time, Zvi Mowshowitz presents a theory
> of Magic advantage that will re-invent the way we think about the game, including a look at all the
> important theories to come before it.**

**Parts one and two together are roughly a third of the total.** The recovered material is the survey and
the critique. The missing material is the construction.

## What the missing parts contain

Every claim below is sourced from a part that did survive. Nothing here is inferred beyond what the text
states.

### Part 3: The Rule of Reflection Applied (2004-02-23)

The Rule of Reflection itself **is** recovered, because Zvi states it in part one:

> **Rule of Reflection: Everything in Magic has value equal to the amount it increases your chance to win
> the game.**

He notes there that he **only found the right words for it after completing all five parts**, and that
**Justin Gary** corrected his phrasing. So part three states some earlier version of the rule and then
applies it. Given part one ends by saying the next step requires separating **tempo, speed, and fixed
resources like life** from cards, and part two ends at *"this is where the classical ends and the modern
begins,"* part three is where the applied machinery starts.

**What is definitely missing:** the worked application of the rule, and whatever formulation preceded the
corrected one.

### Part 4: Land Effect Analysis (2004-02-26)

The title is the clearest signal. Part two contains a forward reference that almost certainly points here:

> **The deck thinning of Land Tax is more important than it looks because you're also giving yourself lands
> out of the deck, making drawing land pointless, so it's unfair to compare it to Ancestral Knowledge
> directly. I'll revisit this at the appropriate time.**

**What is definitely missing:** Zvi's treatment of lands as a resource distinct from cards, deck thinning,
and how land draws are valued under the Rule of Reflection. This is the part of the series that connects to
Block 3.

### Part 5: Conclusion / Putting it all Together (2004-03-01)

Two explicit forward references survive.

From part two, on the definition of tempo:

> **It is important to be careful, because tempo can easily be misused. I'll only get around to defining it
> formally in part five. It's that slippery.**

From the coda, on where the series ended:

> **These ideas can be viewed as an expansion of where I left off at the end of part five of that series.
> The topic at that point was a long term battle between a burn deck and a control deck trying to stop that
> burn over the long term, as an example of how to apply the relationships between resources. However, this
> was an abstracted basic model that has no direct modern application.**

**What is definitely missing:** the formal definition of tempo, the "definitive equations for advantage
calculation" the promo copy advertises, and the burn-versus-control model the coda builds on.

### Also attributable to the missing parts

Contemporary summaries credit the series with introducing **Lineup Theory** — comparing the threats in one
deck against the answers in another to determine who holds the resource advantage. That term does not
appear in parts one or two, so it belongs to parts three through five. Reid Duke devotes a Level One
installment to it at
[block-1-the-spine/23-line-up-theory-2015-03-23.md](../block-1-the-spine/23-line-up-theory-2015-03-23.md),
and Zvi's own [Who's the Beatdown II](../block-2-core-theory-paired/03-whos-the-beatdown-ii-multitasking-2003-02-20.md)
mentions burn used "as a form of lineup" a year earlier.

## Why they cannot be retrieved

**The series was Brainburst Premium.** Every capture of a premium article returns the promotional blurb,
Zvi's tournament credentials, the series navigation, and then:

> **We're sorry, the information you are attempting to view is only available to [Premium Members].**

**Parts one and two survive by accident.** TCGplayer acquired the Brainburst catalogue and at some point
opened it. The Internet Archive's crawl of **2015-01-27** caught both of them free. That crawl is the only
reason any of this series is readable today.

**The decisive check:** of **3,336 captures of `magic.tcgplayer.com/db/` from 2015**, exactly **two** fall in
the ID range 3700–3900 — **3750 and 3777**. Parts three, four and five were never crawled during the window
in which they were free.

### If you want to try again

Three things could still work, none of them from the open web:

1. **Someone's local copy.** These were paid articles in 2004. A subscriber who saved them, or a forum
   thread with a full paste, would settle it. TCGplayer's own forums are the likeliest place.
2. **TCGplayer's internal archive.** The content existed on their servers as recently as the Infinite
   migration. It was not deleted so much as unrouted.
3. **Zvi directly.** He is active and reachable, and the 2004 promo copy says a book version was under
   consideration. He may still hold the manuscripts.

## What to do in the meantime

**Read [01](01-advantage-the-grand-unified-theory-2004-02-09.md) and
[02](02-advantage-vca-and-tempo-2004-02-16.md), then jump to

outright that he wants to take "Zvi's Grand Unified Theory to what I believe to be the inevitable extreme,"
and his three-resource reduction is the clearest surviving statement of where the series was heading.
Flores's [06: The Breakdown of Theory](06-the-breakdown-of-theory-2007-12-17.md) is the other side of that
inheritance.

## Notes for use

- **Do not treat this file as the articles.** It is a map of a hole. Everything in it is sourced from parts
  one and two, the coda, the archived Brainburst category listing, and the publisher's own promotional copy.
- **The renaming discovery is worth carrying.** Anyone searching for "The Grand Unified Theory" will find
  the 2004-05-03 coda and stop. The series is filed under **Advantage Theory**, and it was the Brainburst
  category listing (`article_search_result.asp?report_type=Strategy: General`, snapshot 2004-12-05) that
  revealed all five titles, dates and IDs at once.
- **The general lesson for this collection:** a paywall at the time of archiving is more permanent than a
  dead domain. The Dojo folded in 2000 and is fully readable. These articles were commercially successful
  in 2004 and are largely gone. **What survives is what was free when the crawler came.**
- **Two retrieval bugs cost real time here**, both recorded in
  [architecture-2026-08-30.md](../architecture-2026-08-30.md): `curl -L` on a Wayback `id_` snapshot follows
  the redirect out of the archive to the live site, and a minimum-size gate in a retry loop will silently
  discard a valid capture forever.
