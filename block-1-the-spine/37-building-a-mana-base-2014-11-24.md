# Building a Mana Base

**Reid Duke — Level One, November 24, 2014**

---

## The conversation Duke has every day

> A friend asks, **"Reid, what do you think of my new deck idea?"** and shows me a list of cards.
>
> **"You only wrote down 36 cards," I say.**
>
> **"Well, the rest are lands," my friend says.**
>
> **"Which lands?" I respond.**

> The "friend" can take a variety of faces, including players of every level of expertise. **The fact is
> that building a mana base is the most neglected aspect of deck building. In reality, it should be the
> one you give the greatest amount of care.**

> **It's impossible to see the real picture without understanding your mana base.** Looking at my friend's
> list of 36 cards is **like seeing a painting without a background, or reading a story without a
> setting. You cannot know what's possible and what's impossible before you have your mana base.**

**Duke's attribution, which matters for this library:**

> These are the guidelines I live by. Remember, they're just guidelines, not hard and fast rules. They're
> informed by my own long experience, as well as the opinions of other players and deck builders. **In
> particular, I owe a special thanks to Hall of Famer Frank Karsten's writings on the topic.**

**Why Constructed and Limited differ:**

1. **Constructed decks are 60 cards and play more lands** than 40-card Limited decks.
2. **Constructed formats are faster and more powerful. Being unable to cast your spells on time is more
   costly and your standards for consistency should be higher.**

---

# The scale of colored mana necessity

## The five tiers, with numbers

| Tier | The claim | **Constructed sources** | **Limited sources** |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1** | *"I don't want lands in my deck that can't cast **this** card."* | **effectively all of them** | — |
| **2** | *"I don't want to keep an opening hand without **this** color."* | **17–18** (**19+** to see two in your opener) | **11–12** |
| **3** | **Main color** | **14–16** | **9–10** |
| **4** | **Secondary color** | **10–13** | **6–7** |
| **5** | **Splash color** | **4–7** | **2–4** |

---

## Tier 1: build the whole base around this card

> **Once in a while a card is printed with such steep colored mana requirements that you have to build
> your entire mana base with it in mind.**

**Examples:** a black-green deck wanting **Phyrexian Obliterator**, or a five-color control deck wanting
**Cruel Ultimatum**.

> There's little sense asking **"how much black mana do I want in order to play Phyrexian Obliterator?"
> The wise answer is simply, "All of it."**

> **If you play your seventh land ready to cast Cruel Ultimatum and look down to find a basic Plains and
> a basic Forest in play, no matter what else is going on, you cannot cast your spell on time.**

**The exception he allows:** famous decks with **Nightveil Specter** or **Figure of Destiny** also
playing **Mutavault**, a colorless land.

> **This comes up only when both cards are so important that they simply cannot be passed up. Try to
> avoid situations like this, but when they do come up, recognize the risk you're taking and make an
> educated decision.**

**He notes lands like Urborg, Tomb of Yawgmoth or Twilight Mire complicate the question.**

## Tier 2: mulligan without it

**A deck entirely or almost entirely centered on one color.** *(Example given: Gaudenis Vidugiris at Pro
Tour Khans of Tarkir, a heavily green deck.)*

> **You cannot operate at all if you don't draw this color.**

**17–18 sources in Constructed, 11–12 in Limited. And note: 19+ if you want two sources in your opener.**

## Tier 3: main color

> **A main color is one you're counting on seeing in every game. You hope to see it in your opening hand,
> but you're not necessarily compelled to mulligan if you don't.**

*(Example: Shaun McLaren's Jeskai deck from Pro Tour Khans of Tarkir.)*

> In Jeskai, blue, red, and white are all main colors, **but it's conceivable you could keep a hand with
> only two colors of mana and at least tread water for a few turns while you wait to draw your third.**

**14–16 in Constructed, 9–10 in Limited.**

### The even-split rule

> **When you're dealing with multiple colors you really want to draw every game, you'll maximize your
> chances of drawing all of them by splitting your lands evenly.**

> **If I build a green-white Limited deck, I'll most often simply play nine Plains and nine Forests. It's
> not uncommon to play ten of one and eight of the other, but it would take extreme circumstances for me
> to play twelve Plains and only six Forests.**

## Tier 4: secondary color

> **One you're planning on seeing, but not necessarily right away. It's within the realm of possibility
> to win a game without ever drawing this color.**

*(Example: Pedro Carvalho's Temur deck from GP Santiago, with **Savage Knuckleblade** and **Temur
Charm** as the only maindeck blue cards.)*

> **It can still function if it fails to draw blue mana, but Savage Knuckleblade is an important card you
> hope to cast in a timely manner. Moreover, the sideboard features a healthy number of blue cards.**

**10–13 in Constructed, 6–7 in Limited.** And Duke's note on the example running fifteen blue sources:

> **It's always better to be safe than sorry, and if you can easily afford to exceed these guidelines it
> will only improve the consistency of your deck.**

## Tier 5: splash color

> **Splashes come up often in Limited, but very rarely in Constructed. In Constructed, a color is either
> part of your game plan — in which case play a lot of it — or it's not, in which case cut it entirely.**

**The three Constructed exceptions:**

1. **An activated ability** (one of **Deathrite Shaman**'s two).
2. **A sideboard card for a slow matchup.**
3. **A card you intend to search for** (with **Chord of Calling**) **but want the option to cast if
   drawn.**

*(Limited example: Sol Malka's GP Orlando Top 8 draft deck, centered black-green, splashing white and
blue for one rare each — **Siege Rhino** and **Sagu Mauler** — with three sources of each, and except for
one Plains, all from nonbasic lands.)*

**4–7 in Constructed, 2–4 in Limited.**

# The costs of adding colors

> **Even when you follow my guidelines, the more colors you add, the more chances there are for something
> to go wrong. As I'll say every time we discuss mana: "The more things that can go wrong, the less often
> things will go exactly right."**

## Enters the battlefield tapped

> **A phrase you ought to become very familiar with. It's a very common drawback on lands that produce
> multiple colors.**

**How much of a drawback depends entirely on circumstance:**

- **No cost at all:** a **Dismal Backwater** in your opener when you have no turn-one play.
- **Total cost:** facing a lethal attack with **End Hostilities** in hand, and you draw **Sandsteppe
  Citadel** as your fifth source of mana. **It's completely cost you the game.**

**The four factors that raise the cost:**

> **The costs go up as the format becomes faster or more powerful. The costs go up as your deck becomes
> faster, and as you add more enters-the-battlefield-tapped lands to your deck.**

**Duke's three calibration points:**

| Deck | Tapped lands |
|---|---|
| **Slow black-green draft deck** | **Jungle Shrine has virtually no cost — play as many as you can get** |
| **Slow Abzan in Standard** | **8 comfortably, 10–12 a little uncomfortably** |
| **Super-aggressive R/W Weenie in Standard** | **hope for none, or a very small number** |

## Paying life

> **Aggro decks tend to have no problem paying life in exchange for mana consistency.** This is especially
> important since **enters-the-battlefield-tapped lands can be so disruptive to tempo strategies.**

> **Slower decks certainly don't like to pay life, but they'll prefer it to getting color hosed.**

**And the crossover point, which is the sharp observation:**

> **There'll come a point where adding your fifteenth enters-the-battlefield-tapped land will indirectly
> cost you more life — by preventing you from casting your spells on time — than your first pain land
> will.**

**Why a small number works best:**

> **A small number of lands that force you to pay life is very manageable, because you can use them if
> you're desperate but you'll often have the luxury to use other lands instead.**

**His worked example — about three Llanowar Wastes in the slow Abzan deck:**

> They enter untapped, **allowing me to cast my spells on time**, and they produce two colors, **so they
> help me not get color hosed. However, three is a small enough number that I'm not likely to draw
> multiples and therefore don't have to lean too heavily on them.**

> **If I'm lucky I might go the whole game only tapping them for colorless. Even when I do take a few
> points of damage, it'll mean casting my early spells when I need to, then shifting in the later game
> toward using my other lands.**

## Making a choice: fetchlands aren't full sources

**The distinction between Bloodstained Mire and Bloodfell Caves**, both nominally black-and-red lands:

> **When you play a Bloodfell Caves, you have both black and red for the rest of the game; you're all
> set. When you play Bloodstained Mire, you have to make a choice between a Swamp or a Mountain.**

**The illustrative hand:**

> Forest · **Bloodstained Mire** · Rakshasa Deathdealer · Lightning Strike · Murderous Cut · Sarkhan the
> Dragonspeaker · Nissa Worldwaker

> **Do you want to cast your Rakshasa Deathdealer or your Lightning Strike on turn two? What consequences
> will the decision have for the rest of the game?**

**The accounting rule:**

> **In borderline scenarios it's safest to treat your Bloodstained Mire as slightly less than a full
> source of each color. For the purposes of the above guidelines, perhaps you'd treat four Bloodstained
> Mires as three sources each of red and black.**

> **This isn't an exact science, but it's a factor to consider. It's one reason why Evolving Wilds is
> less appealing than a tri-land in your three-color deck.**

# Putting it all together: the worked build

**Duke plays the role of the friend, presenting 32 spells with no mana base.**

### Step 1 — assign the scale

> **Black, red, and green are all main colors.** I don't automatically have to mulligan a hand without all
> three (**black and red with a Thoughtseize and a Lightning Strike can buy a little time**), **however
> I'll probably lose any game where I don't hit all three colors.**

> **I should be in the range of 14–16 sources of each — and really in the higher part of that range, since
> I need two of each color to cast my spells.**

### Step 2 — count mana creatures, and note the knock-on effect

> With expensive cards to ramp into and demanding colored requirements, **Sylvan Caryatid is a great
> option. I'll count four Sylvan Caryatids as about two sources of each color.**

**But there's a catch that changes a tier:**

> **However, I need a green-producing land before I can cast my Caryatid, so this addition bumps green up
> to being a color I really want to see before keeping my opening hand.**

**Revised targets: 17 green sources, at least 14 red and black.**

### Step 3 — set the tapped-land budget

> **This is a midrange deck emphasizing pressure on the opponent's life total. Not suicidally aggressive,
> but it wants to come out smoothly. I can comfortably play about seven tapped lands, and uncomfortably
> about nine.** Unfortunately **there's no tri-land in this color combination.**

### Attempt 1 — fails

| | |
|---|---|
| **Lands** | 22 |
| **Green sources** | **12** |
| **Red sources** | **12** |
| **Black sources** | **15** |

> **More than enough black, nearly enough red, but not even close to enough green.**

### Attempt 2 — still fails

| | |
|---|---|
| **Lands** | 24 |
| **Green sources** | **15** |
| **Red sources** | **14** |
| **Black sources** | **14** |

> **Still not quite there. So I'm faced with two bad options. Either push my luck and accept I'll get mana
> hosed in some games, or play more copies of Mana Confluence and take a lot more damage than I can safely
> afford.**

### Attempt 3 — change the spells instead

**This is the point of the whole installment.**

> **Let's consider a third option, which is to slightly reconfigure the deck.**

**The changes:** swap four **Hero's Downfall** for a fourth **Lightning Strike** and three **Murderous
Cut**; cut the **Rakshasa Deathdealer** for two **Elvish Mystic** and two **Boon Satyr**.

> **Now black is only in the deck for three Thoughtseizes and a handful of late-game cards. There are no
> double-black spells anymore. Black is reduced to a secondary color.**

**Final mana base:**

> 4 Temple of Abandon · 3 Rugged Highlands · 2 Evolving Wilds · 3 Bloodstained Mire · 2 Forest ·
> 2 Mountain · 1 Swamp · 1 Urborg, Tomb of Yawgmoth · 4 Llanowar Wastes · 2 Mana Confluence

| | |
|---|---|
| **Lands** | 24 |
| **Green sources** | **17** |
| **Red sources** | **15** |
| **Black sources** | **12** |

> **Now I have an adequate amount of green, and more than enough red and black. Whenever possible I like
> to overshoot the minimums a little.**

**His own honesty about the result:**

> **I cheated a little by counting my two Evolving Wilds as full sources of green mana, but at least now
> the deck should be functional, even if it's not perfect.**

### The conclusion

> **Most deck builders strive to make their lands fit with their spells, but this deck only became
> possible when I instead made the spells fit with the lands.**

> **This is why it's a mistake to build a deck without also building its mana base at the same time.**

## Notes for use

- **The five-tier scale with its source counts is the single most useful table in the entire course.**
  Memorize the Constructed column: 17–18 / 14–16 / 10–13 / 4–7, plus 19+ to see two in your opener.
- **The conclusion is the actual lesson, and it's the one people resist:** when the mana won't support the
  spells, change the spells. Duke's build fails twice and only succeeds by demoting black from main to
  secondary. Most players would have shipped Attempt 2 and lost games to color screw.
- **Discount your sources honestly.** Fetchlands are roughly ¾ of a source of each color because they
  force a choice; mana creatures count but can *raise* the requirement for the color that casts them —
  the Caryatid bumping green from main to must-have is a subtle and correct catch.
- **The tapped-land budget is a real constraint with real numbers.** Seven comfortable / nine
  uncomfortable for a midrange deck; near-zero for aggro; unlimited for a slow draft deck. Set the budget
  before choosing lands.
- **The pain-land crossover argument is the best-reasoned small point here:** past a certain count, tapped
  lands cost you more life through tempo than pain lands cost you directly.
- **Cross-reference — this is the Level One bridge to Block 3.** Duke credits **Frank Karsten** by name.
  Karsten's *How Many Lands Do You Need to Consistently Hit Your Land Drops?*
  (`block-3-manabase-reference/02-how-many-lands-do-you-need-2017-05-30.md`) is the rigorous derivation of these
  numbers via hypergeometric probability and a regression on 110 winning decklists, and it separates play
  from draw. Duke's tiers are the usable field version of Karsten's math. Alan Comer's Turbo Xerox
  (Block 3 file 01) is the argument for cutting lands that this installment's conservatism runs against.
- **Also cross-reference installment 02** (Basics of Mana) for the beginner-grade version of the same
  thresholds, and installment 13 for creature lands as flood insurance.
