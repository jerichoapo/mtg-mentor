# Level One: The Full Course

**Wizards of the Coast — DailyMTG, October 5, 2015. Course written by Reid Duke.**

> Why it's in the list: a structured 46-part course by a Pro Tour winner, built to be read in
> sequence. Starts with mana and card advantage, moves through attacking and blocking, tempo, and the
> three archetypes, then into the concepts that decide games: role assignment, damage racing,
> mulligans, sequencing, play-or-draw, inevitability, threats versus answers. Ends with Limited, deck
> construction, sideboarding, metagame selection, and a glossary.

---

## The hub's framing

Professor Reid Duke has dismissed class. Level One is on hiatus, having paved its way through the
entire syllabus to show you how to get better at Magic.

Wizards notes that not everyone started reading when Duke was calling roll back in August 2014. Just
over a year later, many readers were still hoping to grow as players, so they assembled the full
course syllabus for anyone looking to improve.

**The instruction:** start at the top and work your way down. Duke designed his articles to bring you
along one concept at a time. Already advanced? Pick and choose topics to brush up on.

## Corrections to the reading-list entry

**It is 46 installments, not 44.** Counted from the live syllabus on 2026-08-30.

**The free Kindle and iBooks downloads are gone.** Older descriptions of this hub, including the one
that brought it into this reading list, mention free ebook downloads linked at the top of the page.
A full link sweep of the current page returns zero matches for kindle, iBooks, epub, download,
Amazon, or Apple. Wizards removed them at some point. If you want the course offline, this folder is
now that.

**URL prefixes are inconsistent.** The earliest installments sit under `/articles/archive/lo/`, the
rest under `/articles/archive/level-one/`. Several hub links are `http://` rather than `https://`.
All resolve.

## Notes for use

**Where this sits relative to the rest of the library.** Level One is the only item here that was
designed as a curriculum. Everything else is a single argument by a single author. Duke's course is
the connective tissue: it gives you the vocabulary that the Block 2 theory articles assume you
already have.

**The direct overlaps worth reading in pairs:**

| Level One installment | Read against |
|---|---|
| 17 Role Assignment | Flores, *Who's the Beatdown?* (Block 2) |
| 16 Inevitability | Zvi via Flores's *Eight Core Principles* #4 (Block 2) |
| 05 Tempo, 06 Tempo & Card Advantage | Eric Taylor, *it's about TIME* (Block 2) |
| 18 Damage Racing | Flores, *The Philosophy of Fire* (Block 2) |
| 02 The Basics of Mana, 37 Building a Mana Base | Karsten and Turbo Xerox (Block 3) |
| 22 Investment | Flores's *Investment*, described in *Shoulders of Giants* (Block 0) |

**Duke's credential.** Pro Tour winner and one of the most consistent players of his era. The course
is aimed at players who want to get better rather than at readers who want to be entertained, which
is why it reads dry compared to the Dojo-era material and is more useful.
