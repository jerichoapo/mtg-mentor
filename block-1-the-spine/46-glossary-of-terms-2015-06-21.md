# Glossary of Terms

**Reid Duke — DailyMTG, June 21, 2015**

---

## Duke's framing

> **After more than a year with the Level One column, we've covered dozens of important concepts. We've
> also covered many terms that might be unfamiliar to a new player, but which are important for anyone
> interested in learning more about Magic strategy and theory.**

> **This is meant as a reference for any time you might encounter an unfamiliar phrase — not just in
> reading Level One, but from anywhere in the wide world of competitive Magic.**

On the live page, **nearly every term links to the installment that covers it.** The right-hand column
below maps each term to the corresponding file in this folder.

## The vocabulary

| Term | Definition | Covered in |
|---|---|---|
| **Adaptability** | The ability to play offense or defense depending on what the situation calls for. | 10, 17 |
| **Advantage** | Having more of a resource than your opponent, or any other beneficial position relative to them. | 03 |
| **Aggro** | Short for aggressive. An aggro deck (also "beatdown") exploits tempo by playing and attacking with creatures quickly. | 08 |
| **Archetype** | Any recurring strategy with numerous possible variations. | 09, 31 |
| **Blank** | To turn one of your opponent's cards into a dead card. | 03 |
| **Board stall** | The battlefield is clogged with creatures and neither player can profitably attack. Also "creature stall." | 30 |
| **Board sweepers** | Cards capable of destroying many creatures at once. Also "wraths." | 11 |
| **Bomb** | A card so powerful it will often determine the game on its own. Used more in Limited than Constructed. | 30 |
| **Bottleneck** | A limiting factor. To be *bottlenecked on mana* is to have more things to do with your mana than mana available. | 05, 06 |
| **Bounce** | To return a card to its owner's hand. | 05, 06 |
| **Burn** | Spells that deal damage to creatures and/or players. A hallmark of red and an example of reach. Also "direct damage." | 08 |
| **Card advantage** | Any process by which a player effectively obtains more cards than their opponent. | 03 |
| **Card quality** | The ability of your cards to influence the outcome of the game. | 03, 10 |
| **Card quantity** | The gross number of cards you have access to. | 36 |
| **Chump block** | To block in order to preserve your life total, even though yours will be the only creature dying. | 18 |
| **Combat trick** | An instant-speed spell that improves or saves your creature, or otherwise affects the board in your favor, during combat. | 04 |
| **Consistency** | Resilience against things going wrong. A deck is consistent if its failure rate is low and it operates on a reasonable level in a high portion of games. | 29, 37 |
| **Constructed** | Events where you bring a deck of at least 60 cards from home. Standard, Modern, Legacy. | 38 |
| **Control** | A control deck focuses on shutting down the opponent and only wins later, at its own convenience. | 09 |
| **Counterdraft** | Spending a pick to take a card out of the draft so no one else can have it. | 31 |
| **Creature land** | A land with the ability to become a creature. | 13 |
| **Critical mass** | The tipping point at which a strategy that grows in power as more contributing cards are added can be successfully put into effect. | 07 |
| **Damage race** | Neither player can take firm control, and both are trying to win before the other. Also "racing." | 18 |
| **Dead card** | A card with no ability to impact the game. | 03 |
| **Deck yourself** | To run out of cards in your library and lose the game. | 23 |
| **Diversifying** | Intentionally choosing a variety of cards rather than all of the same card for a job — for flexibility, unpredictability, or to attack from multiple angles. | 39 |
| **Dodge** | To create a situation where part of a spell or ability cannot affect you. | 14 |
| **Early stage** | The first part of a game, where tempo is the primary concern. | 06 |
| **Elephant method** | Writing out ideal realistic lists for all matchups, then making the unique cards add up to 75 before deciding the specific 60 and the specific fifteen. | 39 |
| **Evasion** | Flying or any other ability that makes a creature difficult to block. | 30 |
| **Filler** | The unexciting cards that make up the bulk of your deck. Used more in Limited. | 33 |
| **Flexible** | Effective in a variety of situations. A flexible card can be used different ways and helps to some extent in a high portion of the games you draw it. | 29 |
| **Focus** | (1) The most important skill in gameplay: staying present and minimizing distractions. (2) As opposed to a balanced deck, a focused deck is centered on a particular strategy. | 01, 45 |
| **Format** | Dictates which cards you can play with. | 38 |
| **Format-defining** | A card is format-defining if most or all popular decks in the format are warped around its existence. | 11 |
| **Going through the motions** | Being careful and precise with every decision, even the small ones. | 25 |
| **Hate cards** | Single cards extremely effective at beating a particular deck, color, or strategy. | 39 |
| **High-impact card** | As opposed to low-impact. A card with a relatively large potential to influence the outcome. | 03 |
| **Incidental** | Coming indirectly, or attached to another effect. E.g. *incidental card advantage*. | 10 |
| **Inevitability** | A virtual guarantee that one player will win if the game goes on indefinitely. | 16 |
| **Initiative** | The player with initiative sets the pace and forces the opponent to react. The more proactive player has it. | 05 |
| **Investment** | Spending resources now with the promise of a payoff later. | 22 |
| **Late stage** | The last part of a game, where tempo is no longer a big concern and card advantage becomes the focus. | 06 |
| **Limited** | Events where you open fresh packs at the tournament and build on the fly. Sealed Deck, Booster Draft. | 30, 31 |
| **Line-up theory** | Using the right tool for the right job. *Whole deck vs. whole deck* concerns the matchup overall; *hand vs. hand* concerns the cards drawn in a particular game. | 23 |
| **Linear strategy** | A strategy entirely focused on one goal or theme. | 07 |
| **Main deck** | Your primary deck, usually 40 or 60 cards, the same at the start of every match. | 39 |
| **Mana base** | A deck's lands and any supplemental ways to produce mana. | 02, 37 |
| **Mana curve** | The balance between cards of varying mana costs in a deck. | 02 |
| **Mana efficiency** | (1) Ratio of impact to mana spent — choose the cheapest option for each job. (2) Letting as little mana go to waste as possible each turn. | 08 |
| **Mana fixing** | Lands or other cards that can, directly or indirectly, produce more than one color. | 37 |
| **Mana flooded** | When you've drawn far more lands than you need. | 02 |
| **Mana hosed** | When you haven't drawn as many lands as you need. | 02 |
| **Mana sink** | A card that can make use of extra mana. | 02, 29 |
| **Metagame** | The game outside the game. All the decisions, resources, and information — but not the rules — that are important to Magic. | 42 |
| **Midrange** | Built with both offense and defense in mind, with the ability to adapt to whatever situation it faces. | 10 |
| **Mill** | To put cards directly into the graveyard from the library. | 22 |
| **Modal spell** | A spell that requires you to choose modes as you cast it. E.g. Abzan Charm. | 29 |
| **Netdecking** | Copying a decklist from the internet. | 41 |
| **Open color** | In draft, a color not being heavily drafted by the drafters around you. | 31, 33 |
| **Out** | A card or situation that must show itself in order for you to win. | 24 |
| **Overdrafted** | A color or archetype is overdrafted if too many people at the table are going for it. | 33 |
| **Permission spells** | Spells that counter other spells on the stack. Also "counters" or "counterspells." | 12 |
| **Power** | How good a deck or card is without the context of format or metagame. Also "raw power." | 41 |
| **Predicting the metagame** | Thinking ahead about what decks your opponents are likely to bring. | 42 |
| **Priority** | Having priority means it's your turn to act — to either do something or do nothing. | 04 |
| **Proactive** | Trying to progress your own game plan. | 05, 15 |
| **Pushing a tempo advantage** | Leveraging an advantage on the board into an advantage of another kind. | 05 |
| **Reach** | (1) An aggro deck's ability to finish off a wounded opponent even after losing its initial tempo advantage. (2) The ability to block fliers. | 08 |
| **Reactive** | Defending and trying to answer your opponent's threats. | 15 |
| **Sequencing** | (1) The order and manner in which you take your actions in a game. (2) The same, within a turn. | 20 |
| **Sideboard** | After game one, players may swap in any number of cards to better suit the next game. In Constructed, never more than fifteen. | 39 |
| **Signals** | In draft, the cards you pass, receive, do not pass, and do not receive can indicate which colors are open. | 33 |
| **Situational** | Requires particular circumstances to be at its best. The opposite of flexible. | 29 |
| **Slowroll** | Holding the winning card longer than you need to. Malicious slowrolling is unsportsmanlike, but being careful on the final turn is sound strategy. | 24 |
| **Splashing** | Centering your deck on primary colors while adding a color you're not very reliant on, with only a small number of cards. | 30, 37 |
| **Spot removal** | A targeted spell that removes one opposing creature. | 11 |
| **Stack** | The zone where spells and abilities wait to resolve. Typically both players may respond to a spell on the stack. | 12 |
| **Staying open** | In draft, waiting to decide your colors until you've had time to read signals. | 31, 33 |
| **Symmetric effect** | A part of a spell or ability that impacts both players in the same way. | 14 |
| **Synergy** | Cards working especially well together. | 31 |
| **Tempo** | Board presence. A resource derived from how one player's permanents match up against the opponent's. The players' jockeying for it dictates the pace of the game. | 05 |
| **Tier system** | A way of grouping and gauging the relative strengths of the cards you see in a draft. | 33 |
| **Transformational sideboard** | A sideboard plan that has you employing a completely different strategy than your maindeck. | 39 |
| **Turning the corner** | Shifting your focus from defense to offense. | 10, 18 |
| **Two-to-Five Lands Strategy** | Keep your hand if you have between two and five lands; mulligan on zero, one, six, or seven. | 19 |
| **Versatile** | Having a range of uses for a range of possible scenarios. | 29 |
| **Virtual card advantage** | As opposed to *technical* card advantage; accounts for the fact that certain cards have a dramatically higher impact than others. | 03 |
| **Win condition** | A threat, combination, or situation intended to finish the game. Typically used of control decks. | 16 |
| **Zero-sum game** | A game between two opponents resulting in one winner who takes everything and one loser who gets nothing. Tournament Magic is one. Also "strictly competitive game." | 21 |

## Notes for use

- **This is the index to the whole course.** The "Covered in" column maps each term to the installment
  that develops it, so the glossary doubles as a reverse lookup: encounter a term, find the treatment.
- **Several definitions are load-bearing beyond Level One** and are worth memorizing verbatim, because
  they're the versions the wider community uses: **card advantage**, **tempo**, **inevitability**,
  **reach**, **virtual card advantage**, **zero-sum game**, and the **elephant method**.
- **Two definitions have important external antecedents:**
  - **Card advantage** — Duke attributes the phrasing to **Eric Taylor** in installment 03. Taylor
    credits **Brian Weissman**; Flores credits **Robert Hahn's "Schools of Magic"** with first naming
    it. See `block-2-core-theory-paired/04-its-about-time-1997-08-11.md` and
    `block-0-orientation/04-shoulders-of-giants-2013-08-06.md`.
  - **Inevitability** — Duke's definition here is looser than **Zvi Mowshowitz's**, which is positional
    and can flip mid-game. Zvi's is quoted in
    `block-2-core-theory-paired/02-eight-core-principles-of-whos-the-beatdown-2012-03-16.md`.
- **The elephant method is Zvi's**, quoted by Duke in installment 39.
- **Note two terms with double meanings that trip people up:** **reach** (aggro finishing power *or* the
  keyword for blocking fliers) and **focus** (the mental skill *or* a deck's degree of concentration).
