# Draft Walkthrough

**Reid Duke — Level One, February 23, 2015**

---

## The setup

**The culmination of the Booster Draft series** (installments 31, 33, 34). Duke walks a complete draft,
explaining how he used the Level One concepts to inform decisions.

> **I'll be using an actual draft I played recently on Magic Online. It was the Top 8 of a large
> tournament called the Magic Online Championship Series, so the stakes were high and the competition was
> stiff.**

**Format:** triple Khans of Tarkir.

---

## Pack 1

### P1p1 — Dromoka, the Eternal over Pyrotechnics

> **Pyrotechnics is premium removal and would be a fine first pick.** All things equal I'd take it,
> because **taking a single-colored card over a multicolored card gives you more flexibility as the draft
> progresses.**

> **However, Dromoka, the Eternal is a bomb and is a tier above Pyrotechnics and anything else in the
> pack.**

**And the color-commitment reasoning that follows:**

> Despite Dromoka not being black, **the fact that it's both white and green offers a tremendous
> incentive to draft Abzan.** In Khans, Abzan is an excellent combination, **while there's little reason
> to stick to straight green-white and no reason at all to draft Bant or Naya.**

> **After first-picking Dromoka, second-picking a black card is nearly the same as second-picking a green
> or white card.**

### P1p2 — Sibsig Muckdraggers over Whisperer of the Wilds

> **Whisperer of the Wilds is an appealing choice as it's solidly in the strong filler tier.**

> **Sibsig Muckdraggers is not a bomb in terms of mana efficiency, but it can sometimes approximate a
> bomb in the sense that it has an extremely large impact on the game and cannot be easily replaced with
> a different card later.**

> **Card advantage and irreplaceability win out for this pick.**

**Duke later identifies this as his key mistake — see the post-mortem below.**

### P1p3 — Bloodfell Caves over Typhoid Rats

> I strongly consider **Typhoid Rats**, a solid defensive card for the early game. **However, in Khans,
> particularly if you're open to drafting the Multicolor Control archetype, it's good to take early dual
> lands over any card you won't desperately miss.**

> **Taking Bloodfell Caves doesn't mean I'm switching into red. I just want the flexibility to pick a red
> bomb like a Butcher of the Horde if I'm fortunate enough to see one later.**

### P1p4 — another dual land

**Same reasoning: a land over cards he doesn't particularly need.**

### P1p5 — Whisperer of the Wilds

**Strong filler, and an early play good in control and aggressive decks alike.**

### The late second Muckdraggers, and the signal it sends

> **Receiving a second Sibsig Muckdraggers so late is both a great addition to my deck and a very
> promising signal.**

> **The fact that the other seven drafters passed up on the Muckdraggers is a hint that they might not be
> interested in drafting slow, controlling decks involving black.**

### Settling on an archetype

> **Just like choosing your colors, you want to be choosing an archetype.** Archetypes have flexibility
> and it's often okay to blur the lines, **but it is very important to have some end vision for the way
> your draft is going to go and the way your deck is going to end up.**

**The archetype: "Multicolor Control."**

> **Most often based in black and green, but can be three, four, or five colors as you please. The key
> is to pick mana fixing early, so you have the flexibility to pick up bombs in any colors later.**

**Other hallmarks:** **card advantage, lots of removal, and often a delve theme.**

**The two signals that pointed him there:** **the very late Sibsig Muckdraggers**, and **being able to
pick up a lot of dual lands in the first pack.**

### P1p11 — Enhanced Awareness over Typhoid Rats *(a mistake he flags)*

> Card drawing is very valuable in this archetype. **However, early defense is also important. Despite
> taking Enhanced Awareness, in retrospect I feel Typhoid Rats would've been a better pick.**

### Late pack 1

> **I'm hoping not to play any of these cards in my main deck, so I choose the one I might sideboard in
> to answer an opposing bomb.**

---

## Pack 2

### Abzan Ascendancy — a bomb that reshapes the rest of the draft

> Abzan Ascendancy is at its best in a deck with lots of creatures. **Nevertheless, it is a bomb and I
> take it here.**

> **This pick will cause me to put more emphasis on cheap creatures, particularly in black and green,
> which are shaping up to be my main colors, so that Abzan Ascendancy will have more synergy.**

### Duneblast — and reading the pack for what's missing

> **Duneblast is the perfect card for my deck. It's a bomb, and one of the absolute best bombs possible
> for a multicolor control deck.**

**The signal analysis is the best moment in the walkthrough:**

> **Note the signals received from the player on my left here. Duneblast and Suspension Field are both
> better than any common in Khans, and yet a common was missing from the pack.**

> **From this I can infer — not with complete certainty — that the player to my left is not drafting
> white, and is probably not drafting both black and green, as otherwise they'd have likely taken the
> Duneblast as a possible splash. This is good news for me.**

*(The reasoning: the missing card tells you which card the upstream drafter took. If they took a common
over two better cards, those better cards are outside their colors.)*

### The mid-draft self-assessment

> **At this point I feel like I have my powerful late game locked up. I'll be prioritizing early defense
> and smooth mana. I'll also look for a little card drawing to ensure I can hit my land drops and bridge
> the gap to the late game.**

### Rakshasa's Secret over Mardu Hateblade, Highland Game, Glacial Stalker, Dismal Backwater

> **Mardu Hateblade is far worse than Typhoid Rats for my deck because white might still wind up as a
> splash, in which case I can't count on casting it on turn one or two.**

> I go with **Rakshasa's Secret because it provides card advantage and has great synergy with my two
> Sibsig Muckdraggers**, or any expensive delve cards.

### Dig Through Time

> **A borderline bomb, and fits perfectly.** First, **I'd been looking for card drawing, and it's the
> best card-draw spell in the format.** Second, **my deck has a delve theme.**

> **The only concern is that it costs two blue mana, and I'm not sure yet whether blue will be a main
> color or a splash.**

### Passing good cards because he no longer needs that slot

**A direct application of installment 34's scarcity principle:**

> Between **Dromoka**, **Duneblast**, **Abzan Ascendancy**, **Dig Through Time**, and **two Sibsig
> Muckdraggers**, **I already have more than enough late game.**

> **I have no need for Riverwheel Aerialists or Shambling Attendants, despite the fact that they're
> normally decent cards for this archetype.** Similarly, **Enhanced Awareness and Dig Through Time are
> better for my deck than Treasure Cruise, and I don't need more of that effect.**

> **I take a dual land over cards I hope not to play with anyway.**

**And again:**

> **Under different circumstances I might've jumped at Villainous Wealth, but I don't need more late
> game. I badly want Archers' Parapet, but I have little fixing for white mana and need the land more.**

### Abzan Banner

> **Not a strong card, but I don't mind playing one Banner in my Multicolor Control decks, especially
> with as many powerful late-game cards as I already have. Moreover, I need more mana fixing,
> particularly for white.**

---

## Pack 3

### Murderous Cut over Flying Crane Technique

> **Flying Crane Technique is a bomb, but fits well with neither my colors nor my strategy. Murderous
> Cut, as premium removal, is a clear pick.**

### Highland Game — the scramble begins

> **My draft is going well for a number of reasons, but I'm still struggling in terms of early defense.
> Highland Game is the best card in this pack for prolonging the game and ensuring I survive to cast my
> bombs.**

### Committing

> **At this point I'm definitely playing green, black, and white, probably with a bit of blue; I'm most
> likely not playing red.**

**Sultai Charm** is **premium removal and finally locks me in on playing blue.**

### Late pack 3

> **I've given up on red, and just need to take the best defensive cards to round out my deck.**

**And a sideboard pick:** **Cancel**, to bring in against a slow deck with bombs.

### The final mistake he flags

> **Mana considerations aside, Sultai Ascendancy is a better card than Scout the Borders. However, in the
> end I wind up deciding to restrict blue to a splash color, and Scout the Borders would've been a better
> pick.**

---

## The finished deck: "Reiderrabbit," 2nd place MOCS

**Duke's assessment:**

> **This was a super high-power draft deck, able to make use of four rares and plenty of other late-game
> card advantage.**

> **Its weakness was a quick creature rush, as the mana curve was a bit too high and it only had three
> cards that cost less than three mana.**

**The blue decision:**

> **I decided to play a minimal amount of blue. My deck was powerful enough that I could count on having
> inevitability — so long as I could survive I would win the game with or without the blue cards.**

> **Because of this, mana consistency was my top priority and I simply wanted to minimize the number of
> things that could go wrong.**

## The post-mortem

**This is what makes the installment valuable — Duke criticizes his own draft.**

### On staying open

> **As it turned out, my draft would've turned out better if I'd immediately committed to Abzan and never
> looked back. However, much of the draft was devoted to staying open to opportunities, and it was
> largely coincidence that the rares I opened all fit into the Abzan colors instead of touching into red
> or blue.**

*(Note the care: staying open wasn't wrong, it just didn't pay this time. He doesn't confuse outcome with
process.)*

### The classic pitfall

> **I also fell victim to a very classic pitfall, which is that I didn't prioritize the key pieces of my
> deck. I picked expensive cards early, and found myself scrambling for early creatures at the end of the
> draft, often having to pass up more powerful cards in the process.**

**The pick that illustrates it: P1p2, Sibsig Muckdraggers over Whisperer of the Wilds.**

> I took the Muckdraggers because I viewed it as powerful and irreplaceable, **but there's a strong
> argument for taking the cheaper card.**

> **Every single deck wants premium two-drop creatures, but not every single deck wants a creature as
> expensive as Sibsig Muckdraggers.** Moreover, **I ended up with a deck that did make good use of
> Muckdraggers, but I also wound up with plenty of excellent other expensive cards.**

> **I viewed this as a very close pick. Given my uncertainty, perhaps it would've been wise to err on the
> side of the cheaper creature.**

### The two picks he calls almost certainly wrong

1. **P1p11:** Enhanced Awareness over **Typhoid Rats**
2. **P3p12:** Sultai Ascendancy over **Scout the Borders**

> **If I'd had two more playable cards in the Abzan colors, I could've built a more rock-solid deck
> without the need to play a nineteenth land or to stretch for playables.**

### The result

> **I played the deck to a 2–1 record, finishing in 2nd place to a good, aggressive R/G deck** —
> admittedly, **this was my weakness.**

## Notes for use

- **The most valuable thing here is the post-mortem, not the picks.** Duke drafted four rares, made 2nd
  place, and still identifies three specific errors and one structural weakness. That's the model for how
  to review your own drafts.
- **The recurring lesson he catches himself on is installment 34's:** he didn't prioritize the scarce,
  needed slots (cheap creatures) and had to scramble late, passing better cards to plug the curve. He
  states the tiebreaker explicitly — **given uncertainty on a close pick, err toward the cheaper
  creature** — and then shows what happens when you don't.
- **The missing-common inference is a signal-reading technique worth stealing.** A pack where two
  premium cards remain but a common is gone tells you what the upstream drafter took, and therefore what
  colors they're *not* in.
- **Watch how bombs restructure subsequent picks.** Abzan Ascendancy made him prioritize cheap creatures;
  having enough late game made him pass Villainous Wealth and Riverwheel Aerialists. **Card evaluation is
  relative to what you already have**, not absolute.
- **The inevitability argument settles the blue question**, and it's a clean application of installment
  16: with enough late-game power, survival is sufficient, so consistency beats extra power.
- **He separates process from outcome twice** — staying open was right even though committing would have
  worked better, and his loss came to the archetype his deck was structurally weak against. Both are
  honest reasoning about variance.
- **Cross-reference:** installments 31, 33, and 34 are the theory this applies; installment 30 for
  Limited fundamentals; installment 16 for inevitability; installment 45 for Duke's broader remarks on
  learning from your own play.
